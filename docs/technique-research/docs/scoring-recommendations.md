# Coaching and scoring recommendations

Version 0.1.0. No composite performance score or ideal angle thresholds are supported yet.

Start with descriptive observations: turn counts with detection status, duration distributions, boot angular-rate trajectories and bilateral timing differences. Present missingness and uncertainty. Avoid reporting a technique defect when the sensor observes only a timing difference.

| Candidate metric | Research purpose | Main confounders | Condition for coaching use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Turn-duration variability | Describe rhythm in a constant-rhythm task | Intended variation, missed events, terrain | Validated detection and a stated rhythm task |
| Boot edging-rate trajectory | Describe movement development and release | Mounting, speed, snow, orientation errors | Repeatability and an independent task-outcome relationship |
| Bilateral reversal lag | Describe timing coordination | Clock error and event ambiguity | Sufficient synchronisation and task-specific interpretation |
| Left/right waveform differences | Investigate consistency within matched conditions | Inside/outside role and slope asymmetry | Appropriate anatomical reflection and matched-context validation |
| Projected knee/torso movement | Research movement context | Camera perspective and occlusion | Video-only interpretation until independent sensor linkage is validated |
| Insole load trajectory | Explore support and loading transfer | Calibration, boot-cuff force paths and sensor placement | Separate bilateral pressure-validation study |

Reference similarity can be a research feature, but it is not automatically skiing quality. The [Connected Skiing study](https://doi.org/10.3390/s21113779) reports limitations in separating skill groups. Any future score needs independently justified component metrics, outcome labels, population/context coverage, participant-separated validation and uncertainty. Do not optimise an attractive aggregate while its components remain unvalidated.

Use three distinct feedback states: unavailable, descriptive observation, and validated coaching recommendation. Low data quality produces unavailable feedback rather than a low performance score. A valid measurement can still lack coaching significance. Evidence of association alone does not establish that increasing or decreasing a feature will improve technique.
