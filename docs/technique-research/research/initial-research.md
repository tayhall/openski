# OpenSki Technique Intelligence: initial research

Research date: 2026-10-08. Status: initial source assessment and proposed methodology, not validated reference data. No footage was downloaded or analysed. No measured ranges or performance thresholds are established here.

## Recommended starting point

Start with controlled parallel turns on groomed terrain, comparing carved and deliberately steered/skidded demonstrations across short and long turn tasks. Prioritise turn detection, turn duration, boot edging dynamics and bilateral timing. Use body-pose measurements to explain movement context and test hypotheses, not as quantities a boot sensor can directly reproduce.

Effective skiing must be defined against a task: controlling speed and line, producing the intended turn shape, adapting to terrain, or race performance. A large angle or highly repeatable movement alone does not establish effectiveness. Record outcomes separately from movement features.

## Measurement taxonomy and sensor priorities

The following are proposed research features, not established indicators of good skiing. `Video` means feasibility under a suitable camera, not guaranteed observability.

| Priority / feature | Operational definition | Video limitations | Single boot / dual boots / insoles |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1: turn duration and rhythm | Interval between consecutive same-definition turn-switch events; per-direction distributions and within-run variability | Review event intervals; cuts, slow motion and missed turns invalidate timing | Potential / improved bilateral evidence / supplementary loading events |
| P1: edging reversal timing | Candidate reversal in signed boot lateral rotation or angular velocity; keep distinct from ski-flat, load-transfer and trajectory-switch events | Pose alone sees leg motion, not ski-edge contact; manually annotate visible reversal where possible | Estimated proxy / bilateral lag possible / load transfer is a different event |
| P1: edging development and release | Boot angular-velocity trajectory and time of peak boot roll proxy relative to a turn interval | Shank inclination is not boot or ski roll; neither is true edge angle | Conditional orientation/rate proxy / comparison of both boots / no orientation |
| P1: left/right consistency | Compare matched left- and right-turn durations and reflected phase-normalised boot waveforms | Anatomical left/right must survive occlusion; terrain may explain asymmetry | Same boot across directions only / bilateral coordination and between-turn comparison / bilateral loading comparison |
| P2: knee flexion trajectory | Apparent hip–knee–ankle included angle; flexion proxy = 180 degrees minus included angle | Best near sagittal view; frontal views confound depth. Clothing and boots obscure joints | No / no / no |
| P2: leg and torso inclination | Signed ankle-to-hip segment and pelvis-midpoint-to-shoulder-midpoint angles to image vertical or an explicitly calibrated reference | Image vertical is not gravity or snow normal; changing view invalidates direct comparison | Boot inclination is a distinct conditional proxy / same / no |
| P2: apparent torso–leg angulation | Signed difference between projected torso and leg axes in a specified view | Not anatomical hip angle; can confound lateral bend, rotation and perspective | No / no / no |
| P2: flexion/extension sequencing | Timing of knee-angle extrema and changes relative to turn events; signed phase lags | Extrema become unstable with gaps and smoothing; sequence does not establish cause | No direct body sequencing / no / loading sequence only |
| P3: torso/leg rotational separation and arm consistency | View-specific shoulder/pelvis axis relationship; hand paths normalised by body scale | Frontal axis geometry is not transverse-plane rotation; poles and occlusion interfere | No / no / no |
| Defer: ankle flexion | True shank-to-foot relationship requires visible anatomical segments | Pose foot landmarks in rigid ski boots are unreliable; boot pitch is not ankle flexion | No direct ankle angle / no / no |
| Defer: true ski edge angle | Angle between local ski base and local snow surface | Requires ski geometry tracking and calibrated snow plane or additional instrumentation; MediaPipe has no ski landmarks | Not direct / not direct / not direct |
| Future: outside-foot loading | Bilateral calibrated insole load trajectory and distribution | Video cannot quantify forces or reliably infer which ski is loaded | No / no / possible; sensor location, calibration and boot-cuff force paths limit interpretation |

Outside-leg identity must follow skier heading/trajectory and turn direction, not screen side. It can be unknown. Preserve anatomical-side measurements even when outside/inside classification is unavailable.

Useful relationships to test include boot reversal versus visible transition, bilateral reversal lag, peak inclination timing versus peak knee flexion, flexion/extension during release, and within-task waveform consistency. An ankle→knee→hip causal sequence must not be assumed. Compare short/long and carved/steered tasks separately. Treat smoothness as a noise-sensitive descriptive feature; bumps, tactics and necessary corrections can produce legitimate irregularity.

## Proposed MediaPipe pipeline

1. **Permission gate and manifest.** Accept a local permitted file plus a source manifest. Check processing rights and retention scope. Record file hash, pseudonymous skier/run IDs, timebase, speed manipulation and clip interval. Temporary media location is outside the exported dataset. Do not include acquisition or public-video downloading.
2. **Decode and quality review.** Use OpenCV for frames and inspect presentation timestamps with a suitable decoder/container tool. Do not silently substitute frame index / nominal FPS for variable-frame-rate presentation time. Record dimensions, rotation, dropped/duplicate frames and camera cuts. Reject edited or unknown-speed sections for timing. Prefer continuous fixed-camera footage, visible whole body and skis, and separately recorded frontal/rear and side views. Record slope, snow, intended turn task, equipment and speed as unknown unless independently available.
3. **Pose inference.** Use MediaPipe Tasks Pose Landmarker in VIDEO mode with strictly increasing millisecond timestamps. Record package/model versions and model SHA-256. Start with one skier and manually verify identity; reset tracking at cuts. Preserve original timestamps separately from inference timestamps. Export 33 landmarks with visibility/presence, image coordinates and optional model-estimated world coordinates. Google documents both output spaces; neither establishes calibrated skiing biomechanics [S12].
4. **Geometry.** Convert normalised x/y to pixel coordinates before angle calculation so aspect ratio does not distort angles. For a vertex B use acos(clip(dot(A-B,C-B)/(norm(A-B)*norm(C-B)), -1, 1)); reject degenerate vectors. Retain included-angle and flexion conventions explicitly. Image inclination uses signed atan2 relative to a declared upward image axis. Never substitute estimated world landmarks for validated multi-view reconstruction. A ground-plane homography cannot recover arbitrary out-of-plane body joints.
5. **Quality masking and smoothing.** Retain raw and filtered trajectories. Mask occluded, low-detection-quality, implausible jumps, anatomical side swaps and identity changes. Track valid coverage for each metric. Choose filter and gap limits through validation; log parameters. Do not bridge long occlusions or cuts. Offline zero-phase filtering cannot be used to claim equivalent online latency. Landmark visibility is not probability that an angle is correct.
6. **Turn/event annotation first.** Two reviewers mark candidate turn switches with uncertainty intervals and event definitions. Use trajectory change and ski/boot geometry when visible. Model pose proxies may propose candidates for review. Six requested phases are optional, nullable annotations: initiation, engagement, development, maximum observed inclination/angulation, release and transition. Engagement and release of loading are often unobservable. Max inclination and max angulation can occur at different times; events can overlap and must not be forced into six ordered bins.
7. **Features and summaries.** Produce timestamped per-side metrics, reviewed event records and per-turn duration/coverage/extrema with uncertainty. Keep full time trajectories before phase normalisation. Resample only valid intervals; do not hide gaps. Aggregate by task, view and conditions, with skier-level sample counts and distributions, not pooled frame counts as independent observations.
8. **Export and cleanup.** NumPy for geometry, Pandas for tables, JSON/JSONL for provenance/turns, CSV or Parquet for trajectories. Default exports contain no frames, overlays or video copies. Retain derived landmarks only where permission covers derivatives. Delete only pipeline-created temporary copies according to the manifest policy; never delete user-owned originals. A source link and file hash support audit without a permanent media library.

This is a design proposal. No MediaPipe implementation or skiing accuracy result is claimed in this first research task.

## Data contracts

Use separate versioned entities rather than placing everything inside a single angle record:

| Entity | Required information |
|---|---|
| Source | ID, URL, title, creator/authors, publication date or unknown, access date, evidence kind, licence/permission scope and evidence, limitations |
| Claim | Exact paraphrased coaching/research claim, source IDs and locator, population/context, evidence assessment, contradictions, status: published guidance / observation / hypothesis |
| Recording/run | Source ID, permitted asset hash, pseudonymous skier, task and label provenance, camera and timing metadata, snow/slope/speed/equipment with units and unknowns, permission and retention policy |
| Method | Software/model versions and hashes, landmark definitions, coordinate space, reference axes, filtering, calibration and rejection parameters |
| Sample | Recording ID, presentation timestamp, anatomical side, metric ID/value/unit, raw or filtered status, visibility/presence, quality flags |
| Event/turn | Definition, time or uncertainty interval, method and reviewer, direction/heading basis, partial-turn flag, metrics, coverage, limitations and linked evidence |
| Validation | Reference method, synchronisation uncertainty, participant/run split, error distributions, agreement, exclusions and applicability domain |

Unknown values are JSON null, never zero. Units and coordinate space belong to each metric. Use `confidence: null` until a metric-specific confidence model is calibrated. Keep tracking diagnostics, measurement uncertainty, source evidence and label certainty separate. A single invented confidence number conceals these differences.

The adjacent JSON Schema is an initial per-turn interchange contract, not a complete storage schema for every entity. Its example is explicitly unmeasured. Semantic validators must additionally resolve source/method IDs, check rights scope, event/turn time ordering, units for metric IDs, coordinate applicability and coverage denominators. Schema validation alone cannot establish evidence quality or permission.

## OpenSki integration and validation

Log raw accelerometer and gyroscope samples, device monotonic timestamp, sequence counter, boot side, sample rate, full-scale settings, calibration, mounting transform, firmware version and dropped packets. BLE arrival time is not sensor sample time. Record clipping, temperature/bias effects and mounting changes.

MPU6050's six-axis sensing can support angular-rate features and conditional roll/pitch estimation, but acceleration during turns contaminates gravity estimation; heading drifts without an external reference. Ski orientation relative to snow additionally requires the local snow normal and boot-to-ski alignment. Do not label fused roll relative to gravity as ski edge angle. Boot acceleration is not outside-ski pressure or radial force without a separately validated model.

First implement descriptive outputs: detected turns and event uncertainty, turn-duration distribution, within-run rhythm variability, boot angular-rate waveforms and optional bilateral lag. A one-boot prototype needs its own validation; bilateral published results cannot simply be copied. Compare directions for the same boot with caution: they correspond to different inside/outside roles. Dual boots enable bilateral comparison but do not reveal knees, hips or torso. Insoles later add load-location information, with separate validation of total-load and distribution estimates.

Proposed experiment:

- Recruit several skiers spanning ability; collect repeated matched tasks across days, speeds, snow and slope conditions. Record coached intent separately from actual observed outcome.
- Synchronise video and both IMUs using a visible physical event recorded in sensors, repeat at run end, and estimate offset, drift and synchronisation uncertainty. Use an independent measured ski/snow reference if validating edge angle; use body-segment reference measurements if validating joint angles.
- Have two qualified reviewers independently annotate events and technique/outcomes, blinded to candidate sensor feedback. Report disagreement; do not resolve all ambiguous events by arbitrary consensus labels.
- Evaluate turn precision/recall, missed and extra events, event-time error and agreement intervals by style. Check sensitivity to one-boot versus two-boot input, dropout and changed mounting. For angles report bias, absolute error, agreement and coverage against a suitable reference; distinguish manual 2D annotation repeatability from anatomical accuracy.
- Partition development and validation by skier and run/day, avoiding adjacent-turn leakage. Report effective skier counts and uncertainty, not only numbers of turns. Do not train on third-party data without training rights.
- Only advance a coaching metric when it is repeatable, independently measurable and related to a desired outcome within its declared task/context. Compare against simple descriptive baselines. Establish sample-size and acceptance targets from intended feedback use and pilot error estimates before a confirmatory study.

No composite score, ideal knee angle, ideal edge angle or blanket symmetry target is recommended at this stage. Initial feedback can report observations and their uncertainty; prescriptive feedback requires validated outcome relationships.
